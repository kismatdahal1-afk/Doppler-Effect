INTRODUCTION

A shift hiding
in plain hearing

First explained by Christian Doppler in 1842, this effect shows up every time you hear a siren pass by — and it works the same way for light, water, and radar waves too.

What Is It?

The change in the frequency of a wave — sound, light, or radio — that an observer perceives when the source, the observer, or both are moving relative to one another.

Change in frequency

Why Does It Occur?

Motion changes the spacing between successive wavefronts — compressed ahead of a moving source, stretched out behind it.

Relative motion

Why It Matters

It's one of the few physics ideas you use daily without noticing — and it underpins radar, ultrasound, and how we measure the universe.

Powerful real-world impact

Where You'll Hear It

Sirens, trains, radar guns, weather forecasting, and medical ultrasound all rely on it — explored in detail further down this page.

All around you

REAL-WORLD USE

Applications & examples

Beyond the classroom, the same shift is engineered into instruments that measure speed, weather, and even the flow of blood.

Ambulance Sirens

The siren sounds sharp and high as an ambulance approaches, then drops noticeably the moment it passes and pulls away.

Passing Trains

A train whistle rises as it nears a platform and falls as it rushes past — a live Doppler curve you can hear.

Police Radar Guns

Radar bounces microwaves off a moving car; the frequency shift in the return signal reveals its exact speed.

Weather Radar

Meteorologists track shifts in reflected radar pulses to map wind speed, storm rotation, and rainfall.

Medical Ultrasound

Doppler ultrasound reads the frequency shift of sound reflecting off moving blood cells to visualise blood flow.

GOOD TO KNOW

Limitations of the model

The classic formulas are a simplification. Here's where they start to break down.

Assumes Low Speed

Accurate only when source and observer speeds stay much smaller than the speed of sound.

A Medium Is Required

Sound needs air, water, or another medium — the acoustic effect cannot occur in a vacuum.

Wind Interference

Moving air changes the effective speed of sound relative to the ground, introducing error.

Complex Motion

Curved paths or acceleration make the simple straight-line formulas insufficient.

CONCLUSION

One idea, heard everywhere

From a passing ambulance to the redshifted light of a distant galaxy, the Doppler effect is the same simple idea wearing different clothes: motion changes what a wave looks like to whoever receives it.
